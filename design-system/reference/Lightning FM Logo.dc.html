Logo directions · four routes

A mark you own

All four are built from squares and straight edges on the same grid the site already uses, so they hold at 16px, print in one colour, and sit next to mono type without arguing with it. Reference any of them in chat by its id.

1a Step a bolt, drawn on the grid

Three squares stepping down and left. It reads as a bolt without drawing one, so it keeps the association you already own while looking like it came from the same ruler as the borders and panes. The most conservative route, and the one that will survive being embroidered on a tote bag.

1b Signal the music, not the money

Five bars, tallest off-centre, trailing into a single square. Every other bitcoin-music project reaches for a bolt; this one says music first and leaves the payment rail to the copy. It also animates for free — the bars are the player's level meter — which is worth something on a site that should be playing something.

Two strokes lifted from the double colon already in your page titles, set as slashes. It is a typographic mark rather than a picture, so it belongs to the terminal identity completely and can be typed as well as drawn. The trade: on its own it says protocol, not music, and it needs the wordmark next to it to mean anything.

1d Block the cursor, split

The block cursor your design system already blinks, cut on the diagonal so the amber does half the work. It is the only route here that is genuinely two-colour by construction, which makes it the strongest at 16px and the most restrictive in print. Boldest and most abstract of the four.

How I would choose

If the priority is that a musician recognises what this is in one second, take 1b. It is the only one that says music.

If the priority is continuity with the bolt you have been using and the least explaining, take 1a.

If the audience is going to be more technical over time than the copy currently assumes, 1c and 1d age better and look less like a crypto logo.
